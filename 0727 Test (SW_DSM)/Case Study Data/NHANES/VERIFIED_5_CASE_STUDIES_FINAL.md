## 📊 THE 5 VERIFIED CASE STUDIES

### **CASE 1: Smoking Status → Triglycerides** ⭐ **TOP PRIORITY**

**Research Question:** Does current smoking increase triglyceride levels?

**Variables Verified:**
- **Treatment:** SMQ020 (Ever smoked 100+ cigarettes) + SMQ040 (Current status)
  - Current smokers: N = 7,126
  - Non-smokers: N = 28,547
- **Outcome:** LBXTR (Triglycerides, mg/dL)
  - N = 18,149
  - Mean (SD): 117.2 (103.8) mg/dL
  - Range: 10 - 4,233 mg/dL
- **Complete cases:** N = **15,374** ✅

**Motivating Publications:**
- Multiple NHANES smoking-lipid studies
- Well-established cardiometabolic effect
- Policy relevance: smoking cessation programs

**Expected Results:**
- Strong effect (smokers have 20-40% higher triglycerides)
- Large sample-population bias expected (smoking correlates with SES/race)
- Clinical relevance: cardiovascular risk

**Priority:** 🔥 **HIGHEST** - Strong effect, large N, policy relevant

---

### **CASE 2: Physical Activity → Systolic Blood Pressure** ⭐ **HIGH PRIORITY**

**Research Question:** Does regular physical activity lower systolic blood pressure?

**Variables Verified:**
- **Treatment:** PAQ605 (Vigorous work activity) & PAQ650 (Vigorous recreational activity)
  - 88 PA variables available
  - N with PA data: ~41,000
- **Outcome:** BPXSY1 (Systolic BP, mmHg)
  - N = 42,051
  - Mean (SD): 119.9 (19.0) mmHg
  - Range: 66 - 238 mmHg
- **Complete cases:** N = **36,694** ✅ **LARGEST SAMPLE**

**Motivating Publications:**
- Numerous NHANES PA-BP studies
- Public health priority: PA guidelines (150 min/week)
- Clinical relevance: hypertension prevention

**Expected Results:**
- Moderate effect (active individuals 5-10 mmHg lower SBP)
- Moderate bias expected (PA correlates with SES/access)
- Clinical significance: meaningful for HTN prevention

**Priority:** 🔥 **HIGHEST** - Largest N, public health priority

---

### **CASE 3: Obesity → ALT (Liver Enzyme)** ⭐ **HIGH PRIORITY**

**Research Question:** Does obesity increase ALT levels (marker of liver damage)?

**Variables Verified:**
- **Treatment:** BMXBMI (Body Mass Index)
  - Obese (BMI ≥30): N = 14,024
  - Non-obese (BMI <30): N = 38,667
- **Outcome:** LBXSATSI (ALT, U/L)
  - N = 37,912
  - Mean (SD): 24.0 (19.9) U/L
- **Complete cases:** N = **37,428** ✅ **2ND LARGEST**

**Motivating Publications:**
- NAFLD (non-alcoholic fatty liver disease) epidemic studies
- Clinical urgency: liver disease rising globally
- Strong association documented

**Expected Results:**
- Large effect (obese individuals have 2-3× elevated ALT)
- Large bias expected (obesity strongly correlates with minority status/SES)
- Clinical relevance: NAFLD is leading cause of liver disease

**Priority:** 🔥 **HIGHEST** - Clinical urgency, large effect, large N

---

### **CASE 4: Alcohol Consumption → HDL Cholesterol** ⭐ **INTERESTING**

**Research Question:** Does moderate alcohol consumption increase HDL (protective effect)?

**Variables Verified:**
- **Treatment:** ALQ130 (Average drinks/day past 12 months)
  - 20 alcohol variables available
  - N with alcohol data: 20,793
- **Outcome:** LBDHDD (HDL cholesterol, mg/dL)
  - N = 43,840
  - Mean (SD): 53.0 (15.3) mg/dL
- **Complete cases:** N = **19,852** ✅

**Motivating Publications:**
- Alcohol-cardiovascular paradox studies
- U-shaped relationship well-documented
- French paradox literature

**Expected Results:**
- Paradoxical protective effect (moderate drinking → higher HDL)
- **Interesting bias pattern:** May reverse usual direction
- Clinical relevance: cardiovascular protection debate

**Priority:** 🌟 **HIGH** - Paradoxical effect, bias may reverse

---

### **CASE 5: Sleep Duration → HbA1c (Glycemic Control)** ⭐ **NOVEL**

**Research Question:** Does inadequate sleep worsen glycemic control?

**Variables Verified:**
- **Treatment:** SLD012 (Sleep hours - weekdays)
  - Short sleep (<7 hrs): N = 2,875
  - Adequate sleep (≥7 hrs): N = 9,532
  - 33 sleep variables available
- **Outcome:** LBXGH (HbA1c, %)
  - N = 38,516
  - (Already verified in Lin 2024)
- **Complete cases:** N = **11,214** ✅

**Motivating Publications:**
- Sleep-metabolism emerging field
- Public health relevance: sleep deprivation epidemic
- Circadian rhythm and metabolic effects

**Expected Results:**
- Moderate effect (short sleep → 0.1-0.2% higher HbA1c)
- Moderate bias (sleep duration correlates with SES/work patterns)
- Clinical relevance: diabetes prevention

**Priority:** 🌟 **MEDIUM-HIGH** - Novel area, emerging evidence
