REPLICATION 1: Gobbi et al. (2019) - JAMA Psychiatry Meta-Analysis
================================================================

ORIGINAL PUBLICATION:
- Authors: Gobbi, G., et al.
- Title: Association of Cannabis Use in Adolescence and Risk of Depression, Anxiety, and Suicidality
- Journal: JAMA Psychiatry, 2019;76(4):426-434
- PMC: PMC6450286

RESEARCH QUESTION:
Does adolescent cannabis use (<18 years) cause depression in young adulthood (18-32 years)?

TREATMENT/INTERVENTION:
- Cannabis use during adolescence (age <18)
- Various frequencies (weekly, daily, occasional)
- Control: Non-users

OUTCOMES:
1. Depression in young adulthood (DSM-III/IV diagnosis or scales)
2. Anxiety
3. Suicidal ideation
4. Suicide attempt

COVARIATES (All studies controlled for):
- Age, Sex
- BASELINE depression/anxiety (CRITICAL)
- Parental education, SES
- Alcohol consumption
- Cigarette smoking

METHOD:
- Meta-analysis of 11 longitudinal cohort studies
- Total N = 23,317 individuals
- Random-effects pooled odds ratios
- All studies: Cannabis in adolescence → outcomes in young adulthood

ORIGINAL NUMERIC RESULTS:
- Depression: OR = 1.37 (95% CI: 1.16-1.62), p<0.001, I²=0%
- Anxiety: OR = 1.18 (95% CI: 0.84-1.67), NS, I²=42%
- Suicidal ideation: OR = 1.50 (95% CI: 1.11-2.03), p<0.01, I²=0%
- Suicide attempt: OR = 3.46 (95% CI: 1.53-7.84), p<0.001, I²=61.3%

Does early marijuana initiation (before age 15) cause mental illness in young adults (18-29)?

SAMPLE:
- Ages 18-34 (CATAG7 = 4, 5, 6)
- Total N = 22,758
- Early MJ users (<15): N = 2,440 (10.7%)
- Later/never users: N = 20,318 (89.3%)

TREATMENT:
- Early marijuana initiation: IRMJAGE < 15 (binary: 1=yes, 0=no)

OUTCOMES:
1. Any Mental Illness (AMIPY) - proxy for depression
2. Major Depressive Episode (IRAMDEYR) - closer to their MDD
3. Serious Mental Illness (SMIPY) - severe outcome
4. Suicidal Ideation (IRSUICTHNK) - matches their outcome

COVARIATES (Matched to Gobbi):
- Age (AGE3), Sex (IRSEX), Race (NEWRACE2)
- Early alcohol use (<15), Early cigarette use (<15)  
- Poverty level (POVERTY3)
- County type (COUTYP4)

METHOD:
- Survey-weighted adjusted logistic regression
- Controlled for all key confounders
- Used survey design: ANALWT2_C, VESTR_C, VEREP

OUR NUMERIC RESULTS:

1. ANY MENTAL ILLNESS (AMI):
   - Adjusted OR = 1.43 (95% CI: 1.17-1.76), p=0.0011
   - Gobbi target: 1.37 (1.16-1.62)

3. SERIOUS MENTAL ILLNESS (SMI):

REPLICATION 2: Harder et al. (2008) - Propensity Score Study
================================================================

ORIGINAL PUBLICATION:
- Authors: Harder, V.S., Stuart, E.A., Anthony, J.C.
- Title: Adolescent cannabis problems and young adult depression
- Journal: American Journal of Epidemiology, 2008;168(6):592-601
- PMC: PMC2727198

RESEARCH QUESTION:
Does adolescent cannabis use disorder CAUSE young adult depression?

TREATMENT/INTERVENTION:
- Cannabis use disorder by age 17 (DSM-IV criteria)
- Binary: Has CUD vs No CUD

OUTCOME:
- Major depressive disorder by age 24
- Diagnosed by structured interview (DSM-IV)

COVARIATES (for adjustment/propensity score):
- Sex
- Parental major depressive disorder
- Baseline depression at age 17
- Alcohol use disorders at age 17
- Nicotine dependence at age 17

METHOD:
- Longitudinal cohort (age 17 → age 24)
- TWO analyses for comparison:
  1. Standard multivariable logistic regression
  2. Propensity score weighting (IPTW)

ORIGINAL NUMERIC RESULTS:
- Standard regression: OR = 2.5 (95% CI: 1.1-5.5)
- Propensity score: OR = 2.6 (95% CI: 1.2-5.5)
- KEY FINDING: Both methods gave SAME result!
