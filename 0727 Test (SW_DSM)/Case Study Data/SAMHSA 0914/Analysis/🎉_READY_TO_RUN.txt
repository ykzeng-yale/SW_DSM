===============================================================
RESEARCH QUESTION
===============================================================

Does being female (vs male) affect health outcomes among 
adults with substance use disorders?

Data: NSDUH 2021-2023 pooled (N ≈ 9,465)

Outcomes:
- STI: Sexually transmitted infection (past year)
- SPD: Serious psychological distress (past year)

Original paper: Moore & Kapadia (2025), PMID: 40758304

===============================================================
WHAT'S DIFFERENT FROM MOORE'S ORIGINAL
===============================================================

Moore's approach:
- Survey-weighted logistic regression
- Odds ratios (aOR)
- Single method

Our approach:
- 5 different matching methods
- Risk differences (easier to interpret)
- Robustness check across methods
- Full bootstrap inference (B=200)

Both measure the SAME causal effect, just different approaches!

===============================================================
VALIDATION (RUN AFTER ANALYSIS)
===============================================================

In R:

results <- readRDS("Analysis/Results/moore_2025_complete_all_methods.rds")

# Check all methods completed
length(results$STI)  # Should be 5
length(results$SPD)  # Should be 5

# Check all positive (female > male)
sapply(results$STI, function(x) x$estimate > 0)
sapply(results$SPD, function(x) x$estimate > 0)

# Check SPD > STI (higher prevalence)
mean(sapply(results$SPD, function(x) x$estimate)) > 
  mean(sapply(results$STI, function(x) x$estimate))

All TRUE? ✅ You're good!
